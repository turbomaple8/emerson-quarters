Queen Anne vs Capitol Hill for Coliving in Seattle: Which Suits Which

2026-08-17 · Part of Coliving in Seattle

These two neighbourhoods take most of Seattle's co-living demand between them, and they are close to opposites. Picking wrongly is not a small mistake — it is the difference between a year that suits you and a year spent slightly annoyed.

Rooms in both cluster near the citywide co-living average of around $880 a month, so this is not really a price decision. It is a decision about what you want your evenings and your mornings to be.

Capitol Hill

Seattle's densest concentration of co-living, and the centre of its nightlife. Bars, restaurants, music, the highest proportion of people in their twenties anywhere in the city.

It suits you if you want to be out most evenings, you work downtown or on the Hill, or you are new to Seattle and want to meet people fast. It is the easiest neighbourhood in the city to have an unplanned social life — you walk out of the door and something is happening.

The trade-off is that it does not stop. The thing that makes Capitol Hill attractive on Friday is the same thing that makes it hard on a Tuesday when you have an 8am. Noise is not an occasional event there, it is the baseline.

Not convenient for SPU. Capitol Hill and Seattle Pacific are on opposite sides of the city centre. If you are an SPU student, a room on the Hill means a real commute in both directions.

Queen Anne

Residential, quiet, and expensive — with views from the hill that are genuinely part of the appeal.

Apartment rents here commonly run $3,400 to $5,200 a month, which is the whole reason renting a room in this neighbourhood is interesting. The gap between a room and an apartment is wider in Queen Anne than almost anywhere in Seattle, so a room buys you a neighbourhood you could not otherwise reach.

It suits you if you are at Seattle Pacific — the campus is on the north edge of Queen Anne, on the ship canal, and being on the same side of the water matters more than any fitting in the room. It also suits people working downtown, at Seattle Center or in South Lake Union, and anyone who would rather have a calm street and a view than a bar downstairs.

The trade-off is quiet. If you want to walk out into a scene, this is not that. And the hill is real — steep enough that the city once ran a counterbalance up the south face to get streetcars over it, and which side of the crest you live on genuinely changes your day.

The thing most people get wrong

They choose on the neighbourhood's reputation rather than on their own week.

The useful question is not "which is the better neighbourhood" — both are good, that is why they are expensive. It is: what does your Tuesday look like? If Tuesday is an early class or an early meeting and a night in, Capitol Hill's advantages are things you pay for and do not use. If Tuesday evening is when you want to be out, Queen Anne's quiet is not calm, it is boring.

Be honest about the ordinary day, not the good one.

The Seattle geography footnote

Whichever you choose, check what sits between you and where you go each morning. Seattle is cut up by water and hills, so distance on a map is unreliable. On this side of town the ship canal is the thing to watch: SPU sits directly on it, and anything north of the water reaches campus over the Fremont Bridge — a drawbridge that opens for boats, not for your schedule.
