Seattle Intern Housing: Corporate Housing, University Rooms or a Private Room

2026-10-30 · Part of Summer Intern Housing in Seattle

There are four realistic ways to house yourself for a Seattle internship. Most comparisons rank them by price, which is the least useful axis — they cluster closer than you would think. What actually separates them is what you give up.

Here is each one on its own terms.

1. Company-arranged housing

Amazon and Microsoft both route interns through corporate housing providers; ABODA is the long-standing one in this market. If your offer includes housing, this is the path of least resistance.

What you get: it is handled. Furnished, assigned, and you did nothing.

What you give up: choice. You take the unit you are allocated, in the neighbourhood it is in. If that is a twenty-minute shuttle from your office and you wanted to walk, that is the deal.

Who it suits: anyone who would rather spend their decision-making on the internship than on housing, and whose offer covers it.

The thing to check: whether it is provided, subsidised, or merely arranged and billed to you. Those are three different offers and the word "housing" covers all three. Ask HR to say which.

2. University summer housing

UW's housing office runs a summer intern programme with furnished rooms and a minimum stay of around 28 nights; Cornish Commons also sells summer beds.

What you get: terms built for your situation. Nobody is confused by a twelve-week stay, because that is the product.

What you give up: you are living in student accommodation during a summer when you are not a student. For some people that is fine and sociable; for others it is a slightly odd year to repeat.

Who it suits: interns who want the dates to just work and do not mind a dorm-shaped environment.

The thing to check: availability timing. These fill early. Deciding in February is late.

3. A sublet

Someone leaves for the summer; you take their room.

What you get: usually the cheapest number on this page, and sometimes a genuinely good room in a neighbourhood you could not otherwise afford.

What you give up: certainty, and recourse. You are trusting photographs and a stranger's account of the situation, often with money sent before you land. If the room is not as described, or the arrangement falls through in week two, there is no one to escalate to.

Who it suits: people with local contacts who can vouch for the specific room, or a high tolerance for that risk.

The thing to check: everything, in a live video call, before any money moves.

4. A room in a co-living building

A furnished private room with utilities bundled, on an individual agreement.

What you get: the middle of the distribution on cost, and the thing a sublet does not have — the building still exists and is still run by someone at 11pm on a Tuesday. If the heating fails or a housemate leaves, there is an operator.

What you give up: you do not choose your housemates, and you share a kitchen and bathroom. Less space than a studio, more structure than a sublet.

Who it suits: interns arriving without local contacts who want somewhere that functions from day one and someone accountable if it does not.

The thing to check: whether the standard lease terms flex to an internship. Six and twelve months are typical; ten to fourteen weeks is not, and it is a question to ask directly rather than assume.

The axis nobody puts in the comparison

Not cost. Who answers when something goes wrong.

  • Corporate housing: the provider, and your employer has leverage with them
  • University housing: a housing office, with office hours
  • Sublet: the person who sublet to you, if they reply
  • Co-living operator: the operator, who is in the business of answering

On a twelve-week stay you have no time to absorb a housing problem. A week spent sorting out a failed arrangement is roughly 8% of your internship.

One number that moves all four

If you are weighing a Seattle offer against New York or California, compare take-home rather than the headline. Washington has no state income tax — New York charges state and city on top of federal, California charges state. Here you pay neither.

That difference raises the ceiling on all four options above. A room that looked marginal on gross frequently is not on net, which is worth knowing before you optimise your way into a long commute to save money you were not actually short of.
