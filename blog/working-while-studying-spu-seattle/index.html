Working While Studying at SPU: What a Seattle Job Actually Pays You

2026-11-02 · Part of Student Housing Near Seattle Pacific University

Most students at Seattle Pacific work at some point during their degree. Very few run the numbers before they start, and the numbers here are unusual in one specific way.

Start with the part that is different in Washington

Washington has no state income tax.

That is not a minor footnote on a part-time wage. A student working the same hours at the same rate in New York pays state and city income tax on top of federal. In California, state income tax. Here, federal only.

On a small income the proportional effect is large, because the amounts being taxed are small to begin with. The practical version: an hour worked in Seattle leaves you with more than an hour worked at the same rate in most comparable cities.

Worth knowing before you decide how many hours you need. People routinely take on more shifts than they need to because they budgeted using a gross-to-net assumption from somewhere else.

Then the part that is different about SPU

Seattle Pacific runs quarters, not semesters — Autumn from late September, Winter from early January, Spring from late March, each about ten weeks.

Three consequences for working:

Your availability changes three times a year, not twice. Every ten weeks you get a new timetable. A shift pattern that worked in Autumn may be impossible in Winter. Employers who hire students near campus generally know this; it is still worth saying at interview rather than discovering it in week two.

Ten weeks is short and it ends steeply. Midterms land around week four or five, finals around week ten. There is no long flat middle the way there is in a fifteen-week semester. If you are going to need reduced hours, it is weeks nine and ten, and it is predictable enough to agree in advance.

The breaks are real working windows. The gap between Autumn and Winter quarters is two to three weeks with no classes, and the one before Spring is similar. These are genuine full-time stretches if you want them, and they are a better earning strategy than squeezing extra shifts into week nine.

The hours-versus-commute arithmetic

Here is where housing and working meet, and where Seattle's geography does something specific.

A job twenty-five minutes away costs you roughly fifty minutes per shift in travel. Over ten shifts that is more than eight hours — an entire extra shift's worth of time, unpaid.

Seattle makes this worse than it sounds, because distance is unreliable here. Seattle Pacific sits directly on the ship canal; anything north of it crosses the Fremont Bridge, a drawbridge that opens for marine traffic on its own schedule. Queen Anne is steep enough that the city once ran a counterbalance up the south face for streetcars. A job that looks fifteen minutes away on a map may not be.

The practical conclusion: a slightly lower hourly rate close by frequently beats a better rate across an obstacle. Run it per hour of your time, not per hour of pay.

What is actually nearby

From 608 W Emerson St:

  • Campus — three minutes' walk. On-campus work is the obvious option and the one with zero commute.
  • Queen Anne Avenue — about eight minutes on foot. Shops, cafés and restaurants, which is where a lot of student work in this neighbourhood is.
  • Downtown and Seattle Center — roughly ten minutes by car, well served by Metro from stops about two minutes away.
  • South Lake Union — close enough that people working there generally do not keep a car.

That last one matters if you are looking for something more career-adjacent than service work. SLU is where a large share of the city's tech employment sits, and internships and part-time roles there are reachable from Queen Anne without a commute that eats your study time.

The budget this is all feeding

For context on what you are working toward: a room at Emerson Quarters starts at $895 a month, and that includes water, electricity, heat, garbage and high-speed WiFi.

One number matters more than the amount here. Because the rate is all-in, your monthly housing cost does not move — not in January when the heat runs continuously through a grey Seattle winter, not in a month when your housemates are away. For someone budgeting against variable shift income, a fixed outgoing is worth more than a slightly lower variable one.

Apartment rents in Queen Anne commonly run $3,400 to $5,200 a month, which is the comparison that explains why renting a room is how most students are in this neighbourhood at all.

A short checklist before you take a job

  • Work out your net, not your gross — and remember no state income tax here
  • Map the commute honestly: water, bridge, hill, or nothing in the way
  • Tell the employer your availability resets every ten weeks
  • Agree reduced hours for weeks nine and ten before week nine
  • Treat the quarter breaks as the earning windows they are
