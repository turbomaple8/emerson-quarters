Coliving in Seattle: An Honest Comparison, Neighborhood by Neighborhood

Most "best coliving in Seattle" pages are rankings, and most of those rankings are written by whoever wants to be at the top of them. This one is organised differently, because the honest answer to "which is best" is that it depends almost entirely on where in Seattle you need to be every morning.

Seattle is a city cut up by water, hills, and bridges. Where you live determines your commute far more than it would in a city on a flat grid, and the neighbourhood you choose matters more than the fittings in the room. So this is a comparison by neighbourhood, with a clear note at the end about which situations Emerson Quarters is genuinely right for — and which it isn't.

What coliving means here

The word covers a lot of ground, but in Seattle it generally means a private furnished bedroom, an individual agreement rather than a group lease, shared kitchen and bathroom, and one all-in monthly figure covering utilities and internet.

The market rate for a co-living room across Seattle runs around $880 a month. That number is worth carrying through the rest of this page, because the interesting question is never the headline rate — rooms cluster fairly tightly around it — but what the same money buys in different parts of the city.

Capitol Hill

The densest concentration of co-living in Seattle, and the most social. Capitol Hill has the nightlife, the restaurants, and the highest proportion of people in their twenties of anywhere in the city.

Suits: anyone who wants to be out most evenings, anyone working downtown or on the Hill itself, anyone new to the city who wants to meet people quickly.

The trade-off: it is loud, and it does not stop being loud on a Tuesday. If you are studying, working odd hours, or you value quiet, the thing that makes Capitol Hill attractive is the same thing that makes it hard to live in.

University District

Built around UW, and priced and paced accordingly. Large student population, transit that works, and food that is cheap because students eat there.

Suits: UW students, obviously, and anyone who wants a student-heavy environment at the lower end of the price range.

The trade-off: it empties out and refills on the UW calendar, and if you are not part of that world you can feel like a visitor in it. Also worth noting: the U District is not convenient for Seattle Pacific University. They are different campuses on different sides of the city, and a room near UW is not a room near SPU.

South Lake Union and Belltown

Where the tech offices are. Amazon's campus is in South Lake Union, and a lot of housing in this area exists because of it.

Suits: anyone working at an SLU office, especially interns on a short assignment who want to walk to work.

The trade-off: it is a workplace district that people also live in, rather than a neighbourhood that happens to have offices. It is newer, more expensive, and quieter at weekends than the rents suggest it should be.

Ballard and Fremont

North of the ship canal. Ballard has the better bar and restaurant scene; Fremont is smaller and sits directly across the water from Queen Anne.

Suits: people who want neighbourhood character without Capitol Hill's volume, and who are not commuting somewhere the bridges make awkward.

The trade-off: the bridges. The Fremont Bridge is a drawbridge over a working waterway and it opens for boats, not for your schedule. If you cross it daily, you will eventually be late because of a sailboat. It is a small thing until it is a recurring thing.

Queen Anne

Expensive, residential, and quiet, with genuinely good views from the hill. This is where apartment rents run roughly $3,400 to $5,200 a month, which is the whole reason renting a room here is interesting: the gap between what a room costs and what an apartment costs is wider in Queen Anne than almost anywhere else in the city.

Suits: SPU students, people who want a residential neighbourhood rather than a nightlife one, and anyone who would rather have a calm street and a view than a bar downstairs.

The trade-off: it is quieter, and if you want to walk out of your building into a scene, this is not that. The hill is also real — Queen Anne is steep enough that the city once ran a counterbalance up the south face to get streetcars over it, and which side of the crest you live on genuinely changes your day.

What the price differences actually buy

Because rates across Seattle cluster near that ~$880 figure, the money is mostly not buying a better room. It is buying a location, and the location is buying you time.

Worth asking of any operator, in any neighbourhood:

  • What is genuinely included, and what shows up as a separate charge later
  • How many people share the bathroom you would be using
  • Whether the agreement is for your room or for the unit
  • What happens if you need to leave earlier than planned
  • Whether you choose your housemates or they are assigned

None of those are Seattle-specific. What is Seattle-specific is asking them about the right neighbourhood — because a well-run building in the wrong part of this city will still cost you an hour a day.

Where Emerson Quarters fits, and where it doesn't

Emerson Quarters is at 608 W Emerson St in Queen Anne, about a three-minute walk from Seattle Pacific University, on the campus side of the ship canal. Rooms start at $895 a month, all utilities and WiFi included, with individual leases and roommate matching handled for you.

It is a strong fit if you are at SPU — being on the right side of the water is worth more here than a slightly nicer room somewhere else. It is also a good fit if you work downtown or near Seattle Center, or if you specifically want a quiet residential neighbourhood at a room-rental price.

It is not the right choice if you are at UW; the U District is genuinely better for you and it is not close. It is not right if your life is on Capitol Hill and you want to walk home from it. And if you need your own kitchen and bathroom, no co-living building in this city is the answer — you want a studio, and Queen Anne will charge you accordingly.

That is the honest version. A room near SPU is not the best room in Seattle for everyone. It is the best room in Seattle for a specific person, and if that is not you, one of the neighbourhoods above probably is.

Frequently asked questions

What does coliving cost in Seattle?

Room rates cluster around $880 a month citywide. Emerson Quarters starts at $895 with utilities and WiFi included.

Which Seattle neighbourhood is best for coliving?

It depends on where you go every morning. Capitol Hill for nightlife and downtown work, the U District for UW, South Lake Union for tech offices, Ballard and Fremont for neighbourhood character, Queen Anne for quiet and for SPU.

How is coliving different from sharing an apartment?

You sign for your own room rather than the whole unit, the room comes furnished, utilities are bundled into one figure, and you do not have to assemble a group before you can move in.

Do I get to choose who I live with?

At Emerson Quarters, roommate matching is handled for you. Some operators let you choose. It is worth asking either way.

Is Queen Anne convenient without a car?

Yes, for most things. Metro stops are about two minutes from the building, Queen Anne Avenue is around eight minutes on foot, and downtown is roughly ten minutes by car. The hill matters more than the distance.
