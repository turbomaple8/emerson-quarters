If you are working in South Lake Union, the obvious move is to live in South Lake Union. It is also worth ten minutes of thought, because these two neighbourhoods sit next to each other and feel nothing alike.

This is not a nightlife comparison — that one is Capitol Hill's. This is about living in a workplace district versus living beside one.

## South Lake Union

A neighbourhood largely built in the last two decades around the tech employment that sits in it. Amazon's campus is the most visible part.

**What you get:** the shortest possible commute. If your office is here, you walk to it. Newer buildings, newer everything.

**What it feels like:** a district that empties. SLU is busy on a Tuesday at noon and quiet on a Saturday morning, because most of the people in it during the week are there for work. Food and coffee are plentiful and weekday-shaped — a lot of it closes early or shuts at weekends.

**Who should pick it:** someone whose office is in SLU, who works long or irregular hours, and who wants their life compressed into the smallest possible radius. If your weekends are spent out of the city anyway, the quiet is irrelevant.

**The honest drawback:** you will not meet anyone who does not work where you work, and the neighbourhood will not give you much reason to be outside at the weekend.

## Queen Anne

Immediately north-west of SLU — close enough to be a short, predictable trip, far enough to be a different kind of place.

**What you get:** a residential neighbourhood. Houses, a shopping street, people who live there for reasons unrelated to your employer. Seattle Center — the Space Needle, the theatres, the museums — walkable on a decent day.

**What it feels like:** the same on Saturday as on Tuesday. That is the core difference and it is the thing people underrate until they have lived in a workplace district for six months.

**Who should pick it:** someone who wants their home and their office to be different places; someone who is in Seattle for more than the job; anyone who would rather have a neighbourhood than a radius.

**The honest drawback:** it is quiet, and it is a hill. Queen Anne is steep enough that the city once ran a counterbalance up the south face to get streetcars over it, and which side of the crest you are on genuinely changes your day.

## The price difference, which runs the other way than you expect

Apartment rents in Queen Anne commonly run **$3,400 to $5,200 a month** — this is an expensive, established residential neighbourhood, not a cheaper alternative to SLU.

But co-living rooms across Seattle average around **$880**, and Emerson Quarters starts at **$895**. Essentially the citywide rate.

That is the whole structural point: **the room rate barely reflects the postcode, while the apartment rate reflects little else.** Renting a room is how you access a neighbourhood whose apartments you would not rent — and the more expensive the neighbourhood, the larger that arbitrage. It is wider in Queen Anne than in SLU.

## The commute, which is the thing you are actually trading

From Queen Anne to South Lake Union: short and predictable. No water crossing, no bridge, nothing that can add twenty-five minutes without warning. Most people making this trip do not keep a car, and Metro stops are about two minutes from 608 W Emerson St.

This is the comparison that settles it for most people. You are not choosing between a five-minute walk and a forty-minute commute. You are choosing between a five-minute walk and a short, reliable trip — and buying a residential neighbourhood with the difference.

Worth saying what you are *not* trading: there is no ship canal between Queen Anne and SLU. That obstacle sits to the north, between Queen Anne and Fremont, and it is why "near SPU" and "near Fremont" are different propositions. For an SLU commute it does not apply.

## The tiebreaker question

Ask what you want your Saturday to look like.

If the answer is "out of the city" or "at the office", SLU's weekday-shaped neighbourhood costs you nothing and the walk to work is worth having.

If the answer involves being somewhere, with people, in the place you live — Queen Anne is that and SLU is not, and the ten extra minutes on a Tuesday morning buys it.
