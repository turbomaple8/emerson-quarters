Co-living rooms in Seattle cluster tightly around **$880 a month**. Because the headline numbers are so close, price tells you almost nothing — and the things that actually differ between operators are the things nobody puts in a listing.

These are the questions worth asking. Most take thirty seconds to answer if the person runs the building, and produce vague replies if they do not.

## The two geography questions

Ask these first, because in Seattle they outrank everything else and no listing answers them.

**1. Which side of the ship canal is the building on?**

Seattle Pacific University sits directly on the Lake Washington Ship Canal. Anything north of the water reaches campus over the Fremont Bridge — a drawbridge that opens for marine traffic, not for your schedule. If a room is advertised as near SPU, this single question tells you whether "near" means near.

**2. Where is it on the hill?**

Queen Anne is steep. The city once ran a counterbalance system up the south face to help streetcars climb it. Distance on a Queen Anne map and distance on foot are not the same thing, and the gap is widest in the rain — which, from November to March here, is most of the time.

An operator who answers both of these precisely knows their building. One who answers "it's really central" does not.

## The unit questions

This is where most listings go quiet, and it is what determines your daily life.

**3. How many bedrooms share the kitchen?**

**4. How many share the bathroom?**

Not "is there a shared kitchen" — the number. Three people sharing a bathroom and six people sharing a bathroom are different products at the same price.

**5. Is my bedroom private and lockable?**

The answer should be immediate and unambiguous.

**6. Who else lives in the building, and how does that change through the year?**

A building near a university turns over on the academic calendar. SPU runs **quarters**, not semesters — Autumn from late September, Winter from early January, Spring from late March — so buildings near it turn over at quarter boundaries rather than once each September. That is useful to know: it means arriving mid-year is normal rather than odd, and that you will not be the only new person.

## The money questions

**7. What exactly does the rate include?**

Ask for the list, not the word. Water, electricity, heat, garbage, internet are five separate things. Seattle winters run the heat steadily from November through March, so a rate that excludes heat is a different rate in January than it looks in September.

**8. Is there a usage cap?**

Some operators include utilities "up to" an amount and bill the overage. In a Seattle winter that distinction is real money.

**9. What is billed separately?**

The single most productive question on this list, because it surfaces what nobody volunteered.

**10. Is the rate a floor or a flat rate?**

If rooms vary by size, floor or balcony, you want the band, not the entry price.

**11. What is the deposit, and what are the application requirements?**

Cost of entry, not just cost of staying.

## The commitment questions

**12. Is the lease for my room or for the unit?**

Individual lease means you are responsible for your room's rent, not the whole unit's. This is the structural difference between co-living and an apartment share, and it is worth hearing stated plainly.

**13. What are the standard terms, and can they flex?**

Six and twelve months are typical. If your real need is a quarter, a summer, or an uncertain start date, ask directly rather than assuming the published terms are the only ones.

**14. What happens if I need to leave early?**

Ask before you need the answer.

**15. Do I choose my housemates, or are they assigned?**

Operators differ. Neither is wrong; knowing which one you are buying is the point.

## The practical questions

**16. On-site laundry — included, or coin-operated?** In a city where it rains most of the autumn and winter, not carrying wet clothes out of the building is worth more than it sounds.

**17. Parking?** In Queen Anne, on-site parking is a genuine convenience given the hill and the streets.

**18. Can I hold a room without paying?** A hold that costs nothing is the right shape for a decision this size.
