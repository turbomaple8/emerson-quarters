December in a shared unit near a university is the strangest month of the year. Half the building goes travelling, the people still at home have it almost to themselves, and then in the first week of January everyone is back.

It is a good month if you see it coming. Here is what actually happens.

## Why December is quiet here specifically

Seattle Pacific runs **quarters**, not semesters. Autumn quarter ends in mid-December and Winter quarter does not start until early January — a genuine gap of two to three weeks with no classes.

So the student half of a building near campus is largely away, visiting family for the holidays. Their rooms remain theirs while they are gone. Everyone here is on a twelve-month lease.

The other half is not. People working downtown or in South Lake Union are on office calendars, not academic ones. They take a few days around the holidays and otherwise keep normal hours.

The result is a unit with maybe half its residents at home for two or three weeks, with the people who are around on completely different schedules than they had in November.

## What that is like, honestly

**Quieter than any other time of year.** The kitchen is free. The bathroom is free. If you have been waiting for a stretch to catch up on something, this is it.

**A bit flat, if you are the only one home.** Seattle in late December is dark by mid-afternoon and raining. A quiet building in that weather can feel lonely in a way the same building in May does not. Worth knowing in advance rather than discovering it on the 23rd.

**Easier to cook properly.** Shared kitchens are constrained by other people's timing. For two weeks they are not. People who are home tend to cook more in December than in any other month.

## The three things to sort before mid-December

Short list. Ten minutes of conversation in late November saves the whole thing.

**1. Who is actually here, and roughly when.** Not a schedule — just a rough answer from each person. "Gone the 18th to the 4th." "Here the whole time." "Away a few days around the holiday." It takes one conversation and it determines everything below.

**2. The fridge.** This is the one that goes wrong. People go away for three weeks with food in the fridge, and it is still there in January. Agree the rule in November: **take it with you, or bin it before you go.** Nobody wants to be the person who opens someone else's container on the 2nd.

**3. Heat and the building in general.** If several people are going to be away, it is worth a word about not leaving windows open and letting the heat run against an open window for a fortnight. In Seattle the heat runs more or less continuously from November through March, so this matters more here than in a milder city. At Emerson Quarters utilities are included in the rent, so this is about not wasting energy rather than about a bill landing on someone — but the point stands either way.

## If you are home: a decent December

A few things that make the quiet weeks good rather than grim:

- **Use the kitchen properly.** It is the one month it is yours.
- **Get outside in the middle of the day.** Daylight is short enough in Seattle that if you do not deliberately go out between eleven and three, you will not see any.
- **Say yes to the other people who are home.** A quiet building is a small group by default. The people still around in week two of the break are usually glad someone suggested something.
- **The balcony is not useless.** It is wet and cold, but stepping outside without leaving the building is worth more in December than in June.

## If you are travelling: the five-minute version

- Fridge and cupboard cleared of anything that will not survive
- Windows shut
- Anything valuable not left in a shared space
- Tell someone who is home that you are away and when you are back
- If your travel dates change over the break, let your housemates know

## What January looks like

The first week of January is a reset. Everyone is back, and the students are on a new Winter-quarter timetable.

This is worth framing correctly, because it is easy to read it as the building changing character. It is not — it is the building doing what a building near a quarter-system university does three times a year. The useful consequence: **if your lease starts in January, you are not joining a closed group.** Residents' leases start at different points in the year, and the ones who were home through December are the easiest people in the building to meet.
