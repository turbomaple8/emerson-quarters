December in a shared unit near a university is the strangest month of the year. The building half-empties, the people who remain have it almost to themselves, and then in the first week of January a partly new cast arrives.

It is a good month if you see it coming. Here is what actually happens.

## Why December empties here specifically

Seattle Pacific runs **quarters**, not semesters. Autumn quarter ends in mid-December and Winter quarter does not start until early January — a genuine gap of two to three weeks with no classes.

So the student half of a building near campus largely leaves. Some go home for the holidays; some finish their programme entirely and move out.

The other half does not. Interns and people working downtown or in South Lake Union are on office calendars, not academic ones. They take a few days around the holidays and otherwise keep normal hours.

The result is a unit at maybe half occupancy for two or three weeks, with the remaining people on completely different schedules than they had in November.

## What that is like, honestly

**Quieter than any other time of year.** The kitchen is free. The bathroom is free. If you have been waiting for a stretch to catch up on something, this is it.

**A bit flat, if you are staying alone.** Seattle in late December is dark by mid-afternoon and raining. A half-empty building in that weather can feel lonely in a way the same building in May does not. Worth knowing in advance rather than discovering it on the 23rd.

**Easier to cook properly.** Shared kitchens are constrained by other people's timing. For two weeks they are not. People who stay tend to cook more in December than in any other month.

## The three things to sort before mid-December

Short list. Ten minutes of conversation in late November saves the whole thing.

**1. Who is actually here, and roughly when.** Not a schedule — just a rough answer from each person. "Gone the 18th to the 4th." "Here the whole time." "Away a few days around the holiday." It takes one conversation and it determines everything below.

**2. The fridge.** This is the one that goes wrong. People leave for three weeks with food in the fridge, and it is still there in January. Agree the rule in November: **take it with you, or bin it before you go.** Nobody wants to be the person who opens someone else's container on the 2nd.

**3. Heat and the building in general.** If the unit is going to be mostly empty, it is worth a word about not leaving windows open and letting the heat run against an open window for a fortnight. In Seattle the heat runs more or less continuously from November through March, so this matters more here than in a milder city. At Emerson Quarters utilities are included in the rate, so this is about not wasting energy rather than about a bill landing on someone — but the point stands either way.

## If you are staying: a decent December

A few things that make the empty weeks good rather than grim:

- **Use the kitchen properly.** It is the one month it is yours.
- **Get outside in the middle of the day.** Daylight is short enough in Seattle that if you do not deliberately go out between eleven and three, you will not see any.
- **Say yes to the other people who stayed.** A half-empty building is a small group by default. The people still around in week two of the break are usually glad someone suggested something.
- **The balcony is not useless.** It is wet and cold, but stepping outside without leaving the building is worth more in December than in June.

## If you are leaving: the five-minute version

- Fridge and cupboard cleared of anything that will not survive
- Windows shut
- Anything valuable not left in a shared space
- Tell someone who is staying that you are gone and when you are back
- If your lease or your plans change over the break, say so before you leave rather than after

## What January looks like

The first week of January is a change-of-cast moment. Autumn-quarter leavers are gone; Winter-quarter arrivals come in.

This is worth framing correctly, because it is easy to read it as the building changing character. It is not — it is the building doing what a building near a quarter-system university does three times a year. The useful consequence: **if you arrive in January, you are not joining a closed group.** Several people are arriving in the same week, and the ones who stayed through December are the easiest people in the building to meet.
