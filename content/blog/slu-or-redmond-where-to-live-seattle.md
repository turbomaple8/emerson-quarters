Two Seattle internships with the same salary can produce very different summers, and the variable is not the room. It is which side of a lake your office is on.

Seattle is cut up by water and hills rather than laid out on a grid, so "twenty minutes away" means almost nothing until you know what sits in between. Here is what each office location actually asks of you.

## If your office is in South Lake Union

South Lake Union sits just north of downtown and holds a large share of the city's tech employment. Amazon's campus is the most visible part of it.

Queen Anne is immediately north-west of SLU. The practical consequences:

- **No water crossing.** You are travelling a couple of miles inside the same side of the city.
- **Predictable, which matters more than fast.** No drawbridge, no lake, nothing that can add twenty-five minutes without warning.
- **A car is usually more trouble than it is worth.** Metro stops are a couple of minutes from the building and SLU parking costs more than it solves. Most people making this commute do not keep one.

This is the straightforward case. If your office is in SLU, your housing decision is mostly about the room rather than the route.

## If your office is in Redmond

Redmond is across Lake Washington. Microsoft and much of the Eastside are there. That means the **520 bridge**, twice a day, every day.

There is one genuine advantage and it is counterintuitive: **you would be reverse-commuting.** The heavy morning flow is westbound, *into* Seattle. Going eastbound in the morning and westbound in the evening puts you against the queue rather than in it. You will spend a fair amount of your commute looking across the barrier at traffic that is not moving.

That is real, and it is better than the alternative. But be honest about the rest:

- It is still a bridge crossing, and a bridge has no alternative route when something goes wrong
- It is twice a day for the length of your stay
- Your evenings are shaped by it in a way an SLU commute's are not

If your office is in Redmond, the decision is genuinely open: living on the Eastside removes the bridge but puts you away from the city you came to be in. Plenty of people choose the bridge deliberately. Just choose it rather than discovering it.

## If your office is downtown

Downtown Seattle and Seattle Center are both around ten minutes by car from Queen Anne, and well served by bus. Seattle Center — the Space Needle, the theatres, the museums — is close enough to walk on a decent day.

Queen Anne is one of the few residential neighbourhoods genuinely close to the commercial core without being inside it, which is the trade most people want and rarely find at a room price.

## The number that settles most of this

Over a twelve-week internship, **forty-five minutes each way is about ninety hours** — more than two full working weeks of your time in Seattle spent travelling.

Put that against the monthly difference between a cheap room far out and a closer one. On a stay this short, the arithmetic is rarely close. Paying somewhat more to be close is usually the better trade, and the mistake people make is optimising for rent and inheriting a commute they then live with for three months.

## The two obstacles nobody mentions in a listing

Whatever office you are heading to, check these before you commit to an address.

**The ship canal.** Seattle Pacific University sits directly on it, and the Fremont Bridge crosses it. That bridge is a drawbridge over a working waterway — it opens for boats, not for your schedule. A room that looks a short walk from somewhere on the other bank can cost you twenty-five minutes on a morning when a sailboat is coming through.

**The hill.** Queen Anne is steep enough that the city once built a counterbalance system up the south face to help streetcars climb it. Distance on a Queen Anne map is not distance on foot, and the gap is widest when you are carrying something or the weather is bad.

608 W Emerson St sits on the campus side of the canal and off the steep face. That is a smaller sentence than it is to live with.

## One piece of Seattle arithmetic in your favour

If you are weighing a Seattle offer against New York or California, compare take-home, not the headline.

**Washington has no state income tax.** New York charges state *and* city income tax on top of federal; California charges state. Here you pay neither. On an intern salary that difference is real money, and it lands in your account rather than in a calculation you never see.

Seattle rents look high until you run that comparison, at which point the ordering frequently changes.
