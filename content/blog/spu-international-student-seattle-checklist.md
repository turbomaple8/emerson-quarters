Arriving from another country to study at Seattle Pacific is a different problem from moving across one. You are deciding on housing you cannot visit, in a city whose geography does not read correctly on a map, on an academic calendar most international advice does not account for.

This is the order things actually need to happen in.

## First: understand that SPU runs on quarters

Almost every "studying in the US" guide is written for semester schools — a long autumn term from August, a spring term from January. Seattle Pacific does not work that way.

SPU runs **quarters**: Autumn from late September, Winter from early January, Spring from late March. Three starts a year, each about ten weeks.

Two consequences that matter before you book anything:

**Your search window is earlier than the term date suggests.** If you are starting Winter quarter in January, the time to be looking is **late October through November** — not late December. Rooms within walking distance of campus go to people who searched a quarter ahead.

**A ten-week term does not match a six- or twelve-month lease.** This is the single most common planning mistake. Your academic commitment arrives in ten-week blocks; housing does not. Decide early whether you are staying through summer, because a twelve-month lease signed in September runs through a summer you may not be in Seattle for.

## Second: learn the two obstacles before you pick a neighbourhood

A map will mislead you about Seattle. Two things do not show up on it.

**The ship canal.** SPU sits directly on the Lake Washington Ship Canal. Fremont is on the opposite bank. A room in Fremont can be half a mile from campus and still cost twenty-five minutes, because the Fremont Bridge is a drawbridge over a working waterway — it opens for boats, not for your 8am class. Over three quarters, this will happen to you.

**The hill.** Queen Anne rises sharply; the city once built a counterbalance system up the south face to get streetcars over it. Six blocks across flat ground and six blocks up the hill are not the same journey, especially carrying groceries in January.

The practical test for any "near SPU" listing: **same side of the canal, off the steep face, under ten minutes on foot.** The set of buildings meeting all three is small, which is exactly why searching early matters more here than at a campus on a grid.

## Third: decide furnished or not before you book a flight

If you are flying in with suitcases, an empty room is a problem that takes two weeks and several hundred dollars to solve — bed, mattress, desk, chair, storage, lamp, bedding — before you can properly sleep there. At the end, you sell it for a fraction or give it away.

A furnished room removes that entirely. At Emerson Quarters the room comes with a bed, desk, chair, nightstand, storage and a wall-mounted TV, with a shared full kitchen in the unit. You arrive with a suitcase and leave with one.

For a stay measured in quarters, that is almost always the better trade.

## Fourth: the money question, answered plainly

Rooms at Emerson Quarters start at **$895 a month**, including water, electricity, heat, garbage and high-speed WiFi — one number, no separate accounts to open in your name as a new arrival with no local credit history.

For comparison: apartment rents in Queen Anne commonly run **$3,400 to $5,200 a month**. That gap is the entire reason renting by the room exists in this neighbourhood.

You sign an individual lease for your own room and are responsible for your room's rent, not the whole unit's.

## The first week on the ground

In rough order of urgency:

1. **Campus first.** Register, get your student ID, confirm your enrolment in person. Everything else is easier with it.
2. **Phone number.** A local number unblocks almost every other setup step.
3. **Bank account.** Bring the documents your bank asks for; this is usually the slowest item.
4. **Walk your route to campus once, deliberately.** Find out how long it actually takes, in the weather, with a bag.
5. **Shelf in the fridge, shelf in a cupboard.** If you are in a shared unit, claiming your own space in week one prevents most of the friction that otherwise builds over a quarter.
6. **Transit card.** Metro stops are a couple of minutes from the building; a card is worth having before you need it.

## What nobody tells you about the weather

Seattle winters are not cold in a dramatic way. They are grey and damp, more or less continuously, from November through March. The rain is light and constant rather than heavy.

Two practical consequences for an international student: you need somewhere for wet coats and shoes to dry, and it gets dark early enough that the walk home from an afternoon class is in the dark by December. On-site laundry and a walk measured in minutes rather than a bus ride matter more here than they would in a drier, brighter city.
