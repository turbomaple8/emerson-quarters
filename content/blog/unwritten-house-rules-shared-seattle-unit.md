Every shared unit runs on rules. Almost none of them are written anywhere. They are absorbed in the first fortnight by watching what other people do, and the people who absorb them quickly have an easier year than the people who do not.

Here are the ones that actually matter — not a list to post on the fridge, but the things that are already true before you arrive.

## The ones about shared space

**Leave it as you found it, within the hour.** Not "eventually". The reason shared kitchens fail is almost never a big conflict; it is a pan left "for later" four times in a week. The norm is not cleanliness, it is *latency*.

**Your food is yours and everyone knows which.** A shelf in the fridge and a cupboard section, claimed in the first week. This is the single highest-return ten minutes of a shared tenancy. Ambiguity is what creates resentment, not scarcity.

**Counter space is borrowed, not owned.** If you cook something elaborate, the counter goes back to neutral afterwards.

**The bathroom gets reset.** Hair out of the drain, surfaces not soaked. Thirty seconds that nobody notices when you do it and everyone notices when you do not.

## The ones about noise and hours

**Different hours are fine; unannounced different hours are not.** A building three minutes from Seattle Pacific holds students on a quarter timetable and people commuting to downtown or South Lake Union on office hours. Those schedules barely overlap, which is why it works. What makes it stop working is someone's six-o'clock start arriving as a surprise in week six instead of as information in week one.

**Late is fine, loud is the question.** Coming in at one is unremarkable. Doing it with volume is the thing.

**Headphones in shared space after about ten.** Nobody will ask you. Everyone will notice.

## The ones about the building in winter

Specific to here, and specific to the season.

**Wet things have a place and it is not the hallway.** It rains lightly and continuously from November through March. Coats, shoes, bags — all damp most days. Whatever spot the unit has settled on, use it. If there is not one yet, you are the person who gets to suggest it, and November is the month to do it.

**Do not run the heat against an open window.** The heat runs more or less continuously through a Seattle winter. The rate at Emerson Quarters includes water, electricity, heat and garbage, so no bill lands on anyone — which makes this a courtesy rather than a cost argument, and it is still the norm.

**On-site laundry is shared, so do not leave a finished load.** The building has it, which means wet clothes never leave the building or go up the Queen Anne hill — but a machine with someone's dry washing sitting in it is the most reliable way to annoy a housemate.

## The ones about people

**Guests are fine; guests who become residents are not.** Every unit has a line here and almost nobody states it. Ask the policy early — it varies by operator, and it is better as a question than as a discovery.

**You are not obliged to be friends.** Shared living works perfectly well between people who are merely considerate. Forcing closeness in week one more often produces polite avoidance by week six. Aim for easy.

**Say hello properly, once.** The difference between a unit where people nod and a unit where people talk is usually one deliberate conversation in the first fortnight.

## The two worth saying out loud

Everything above is absorbed. These two are worth actually saying, because they cannot be inferred:

**1. "Here is roughly when I leave and when I'm back."**

Not a schedule — a shape. It resolves the bathroom and kitchen timing before either becomes a topic.

**2. "Here are the days that will be different."**

Finals, which on a ten-week quarter arrive around week ten and arrive three times a year. A deadline week. An early start you do not normally have. Said in advance this is information; said in the moment it sounds like a complaint.

## What makes all of it workable

A private room that locks.

None of these norms require anyone to be a saint, because nobody has to be in shared space when they do not want to be. The room at Emerson Quarters is furnished — bed, desk, chair, nightstand, storage, wall-mounted TV — and the door is the thing that makes the shared half of the arrangement tolerable on a bad week.

And the rules do not extend to money. You sign for your own room and pay your own rent, not the unit's, at **$895 a month** with utilities and WiFi included. There is no bill to split, so there is no unwritten rule about splitting it — which removes the one source of shared-house friction that genuinely does not resolve itself.
