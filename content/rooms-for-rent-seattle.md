# Furnished Rooms for Rent in Seattle: What $895 Gets You in Queen Anne

Renting a room instead of an apartment is a decision people usually arrive at through arithmetic rather than preference. You look at what a one-bedroom costs in the neighbourhood you want, you look at what you earn, and the numbers do not meet. So the question becomes what you can get for a fraction of that, and whether it is somewhere you actually want to live.

This page answers that directly for one building in Queen Anne, Seattle: what $895 a month covers, what it does not, and who it genuinely suits.

## The Queen Anne problem, in numbers

Queen Anne is one of Seattle's most desirable neighbourhoods and it is priced accordingly. Apartment rents here commonly land somewhere between **$3,400 and $5,200 a month**.

That figure is the reason room rentals exist in this neighbourhood at all. For most people arriving in Seattle — students, people early in a career, anyone on a fixed stipend — a Queen Anne apartment is not a stretch, it is simply out of reach. The realistic options narrow to three:

**Live somewhere cheaper and commute in.** Perfectly reasonable, and it is what a lot of people do. The cost is time, and in Seattle that time is unpredictable: the Fremont Bridge is a drawbridge that opens for marine traffic, and the hill means a route that looks short on a map is not always short on foot.

**Split an apartment.** Cheaper per person, and it works. But you take on an apartment's overhead — a lease signed jointly, furniture to buy and eventually move, utility accounts in somebody's name, and a group decision every time something needs replacing.

**Rent a room.** You get the neighbourhood at a fraction of the price, and you give up the extra space an apartment would have given you.

Across Seattle, the average co-living room runs around **$880 a month**. Emerson Quarters starts at **$895** — essentially city market rate, in a neighbourhood where a whole apartment costs several times that.

## What $895 actually covers

The rate includes:

- **A private, furnished bedroom.** Bed, desk, chair, nightstand, storage, and a wall-mounted TV. The room is ready the day you arrive.
- **All utilities.** Water, electricity, heat, and garbage. No accounts to open in your name, no bills split with anyone, no meter readings.
- **High-speed WiFi.** Included, already working.
- **On-site laundry.** In the building.
- **Access to a full kitchen.** Stove, refrigerator, microwave, granite counters, shared with the others in your unit.

What it means practically: you can arrive with a suitcase. There is no furniture to buy, nothing to assemble, no utility deposit, no internet installation appointment, and nothing to sell again when you leave.


## The room, and the unit around it

The bedroom is yours alone and it locks. The kitchen and the bathroom are shared with the others in your unit.

That is the honest trade. You are not renting a studio and you should not expect one. What you are renting is a private room with your own furniture and your own door, attached to a kitchen and bathroom you share with a small number of people rather than a whole building.

Many units have private balconies with views out over the neighbourhood — which in a city where outdoor space is scarce and winters are long is worth more than it sounds on a list. On-site parking is available, first-come and first-served, which in Queen Anne is a genuine convenience given what street parking is like on the hill.


## Where the building sits

608 W Emerson St is on the north slope of Queen Anne, on the same side of the ship canal as Seattle Pacific University, about a three-minute walk from campus.

From the building: Queen Anne Avenue's shops and restaurants are roughly eight minutes on foot, Metro stops about two minutes, Fremont a short hop across the bridge, and downtown Seattle and Seattle Center around ten minutes by car.

The location detail that matters most is the one that does not show up on a map: you are on the campus side of the water and off the steep face of the hill. Getting to SPU does not involve a drawbridge, and getting home does not involve the climb.

## Who this works for — and who it doesn't

**It works if** you want to live in Queen Anne on a budget that would not otherwise reach it; you are studying at SPU or working in or near downtown; you are new to Seattle and want somewhere that functions from day one; or you want a fixed monthly number instead of a rent payment plus four variable bills.

**It does not work if** you need your own kitchen and bathroom, you are moving with a partner or family, you have a lot of possessions, or you want to choose exactly who you live with. Those are real requirements and this is not the right product for them. A studio or a one-bedroom is, and it will cost what Queen Anne charges for one.
